01 Project Overview

This project is building an autonomous algorithmic trading income stream. Starting from zero in April 2026, George (a NanoClaw AI agent) runs nightly research sessions, proposes testable hypotheses, backtests them against 20+ years of market data, and graduates confirmed strategies into paper trading on Alpaca. The goal is live trading of a production portfolio generating consistent after-tax income.

The strategy is evidence-first: every assumption about alpha must be falsifiable and tested on out-of-sample data before any capital is allocated. The pipeline is deliberately skeptical — more than half of all hypotheses tested have been rejected.

03 Discovery Engine — The Dream Cycle

Every night while Kevin sleeps, George runs a two-phase autonomous research cycle:

Phase 1: Wiki Expansion (11 PM CT)

George identifies the thinnest section of the knowledge wiki — the section with the fewest pages and least cross-linking — and expands it. This ensures the knowledge base grows systematically, not just in hot areas. The wiki now spans 50+ pages across 4 top-level sections: Algorithms, Tools, Data Sources, Prediction Markets, Backtesting, and Paper Trading.

Phase 2: arXiv & GitHub Scan (11 PM CT)

George runs a structured 5-angle scan across recent academic papers and open-source repositories:

  • arXiv cs.LG + q-fin: recent ML/trading papers (last 30 days)
  • arXiv q-fin.TR: trading-specific theoretical and empirical work
  • GitHub: new repos with momentum/quant/trading tags, 100+ stars
  • Targeted search: papers directly related to open hypothesis families (PEAD, regime detection, RL trading)
  • Cross-reference scan: citing papers for confirmed hypotheses

Promising findings are staged as JSON proposal files in dream_cycle/staged/YYYY-MM-DD/ with risk levels (low/medium/high).

Phase 3: Build Phase (4 AM CT)

The staged proposals are applied: low-risk changes (wiki updates) immediately; medium-risk changes (new run_hNNN.py stubs) with backup; high-risk changes flagged for Kevin's review. A changelog is committed to git and a session summary published to here.now.

Scale of autonomous research Since April 2026, this pipeline has reviewed 400+ academic papers, noted 80+ GitHub repositories, and generated 371 hypothesis proposals — all without human intervention between sessions.

Infrastructure

  • Data: yfinance EOD prices (primary), Polygon.io (intraday), FRED (macro), FMP (fundamentals), SEC EDGAR (8-K filings)
  • Framework: Pure Python + pandas + numpy; custom vectorized backtesting per strategy type
  • Scripts: All strategies in backtesting/daily/run_hNNN.py; paper trading in backtesting/paper_trading/
  • Costs: 10bps per trade modeled; no leverage unless specified; dividends included in total return
  • Tax modeling: All reported Sharpe ratios are pre-tax (gross); after-tax modeling documented separately; monthly-rebalancing strategies incur ~37% STCG rate → rule of thumb: 1.5–2× gross return needed to match a buy-and-hold after-tax

08 Key Lessons Learned

The most common failure mode (seen 15+ times) A strategy looks great IS but has a walk-forward ratio <1.0 — meaning it was overfit to in-sample data. Strict pre-registration of gates before running backtests is the only defense.

What Works

  • Monthly cross-sectional momentum on diversified universes — The H026/H041a/H045 core has been stable across every regime since 2004.
  • Orthogonal daily signals — IBS on tech ETFs has Corr(H026)=0.21 and is the single biggest Sharpe contributor in the production portfolio.
  • Order Block (OB) filter as universal gate — Consistently improves Sharpe on every tested universe; robust across parameter ranges.
  • Regime overlays for drawdown control — SPY 200MA overlay and VIX<20 gate both reduce MaxDD substantially without material Sharpe loss.
  • NLP on primary source documents — FinBERT on EDGAR 8-K filings (not curated data, not summaries) delivers genuine WR 81.8% OOS.

What Doesn't Work

  • Statistical pairs trading on ETFs — In-sample cointegration was anti-predictive of out-of-sample performance across all tested pairs.
  • Yield curve timing overlays — 2022–24 inversion = prolonged false positive; already captured by momentum organically.
  • Adding too many features to NLP models — Speaker weighting, item-specific text, EPS surprise filters all hurt by reducing sample size or introducing availability bias.
  • Sector-neutral adjustments on concentrated US universes — Works globally; on concentrated S&P 500 large-cap universes, neutralization increases SPY correlation and destroys alpha.
  • Combining strategies with Corr > 0.70 — No blending benefit; one strategy's capital is better deployed into the higher-Sharpe option.

Structural Findings

  • The OB universality result is the most surprising finding in the project: a single set of ICT technical parameters improves OOS Sharpe on stocks, sector ETFs, bond ETFs, and low-vol factor ETFs.
  • Survivorship bias matters most in stock strategies (~+4.9pp returns per Ranse 2025). ETF rotation is largely immune.
  • The 2022 bear market is the single most informative OOS test. Strategies that held up in 2022 have genuine risk-adjusted edge; strategies that failed often had hidden duration exposure or equity beta.
