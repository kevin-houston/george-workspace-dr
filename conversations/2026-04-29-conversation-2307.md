**George**: **OOS — Out-of-Sample**
When backtesting, you split history into two blocks: the *in-sample* (IS) period is used to develop the strategy, and the *out-of-sample* (OOS) period is kept locked away until the end as an honest reality check. If a strategy looks great IS but falls apart OOS, it was just curve-fitted to the past. OOS is the number that actually matters — it's the closest thing to live performance you can get from historical data.

**IBS — Internal Bar Strength**
A simple daily indicator: `IBS = (Close − Low) / (High − Low)`. It measures where price closed within the day's range. IBS near 0 means price closed near the day's *low* (got beaten up all day — oversold). IBS near 1 means it closed near the *high*. We use it as a mean-reversion signal: buy when IBS < threshold (e.g., 0.20) because beaten-up stocks tend to bounce the next day, sell when IBS > 0.75 because overbought stocks tend to pull back. The XLK/SMH/IGV components of the portfolio use this as a short-term trading overlay on top of the longer monthly rotation strategies.
