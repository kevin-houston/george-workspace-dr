**arXiv:2502.07606 — Kearns & Shi (2025)**

This one's algorithmic game theory for trade *execution*, not a signal paper — so it won't generate alpha directly, but it's intellectually serious and has some practical implications.

**What it actually is**

Multi-player execution game: N traders each need to buy/sell a fixed number of shares over a fixed window, independently minimizing their own market impact cost. The framework decomposes impact into:

- **Temporary**: you pay for competing with whoever else is trading *right now* → optimal response is to spread your order across time windows others aren't using
- **Permanent**: you pay based on everyone's *accumulated* position before your trade → optimal response is to trade *before* others (front-running)

κ controls the mix. Real markets have both, creating the tension the paper analyzes.

The temporary-impact-only game is a *potential game* — best-response dynamics converge to Nash equilibrium. The permanent-impact-only game is zero-sum — no known efficient NE algorithm. Their algorithm — FTPL (Follow the Perturbed Leader) — achieves CCE and is a no-regret learner: if you run it, your long-run average cost is competitive regardless of what other players do.
