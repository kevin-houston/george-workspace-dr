## Focus 1: Metric Shifting as Bearish Alpha Signal (arXiv:2510.03195)

### Paper Details
- **Title**: From Text to Alpha: Can LLMs Track Evolving Signals in Corporate Disclosures?
- **Authors**: Chanyeol Choi, Yoon Kim, Yu Yu, Young Cha, V. Zach Golkhou, Igor Halperin, Georgios Papaioannou, Minkyu Kim, Zhangyang Wang, Jihoon Kwon, Minjae Kim, Alejandro Lopez-Lira, Yongjae Lee
- **Institutions**: LinqAlpha, MIT, BlackRock, Blackstone, J.P. Morgan, Fidelity, Qube Research & Technologies, State Street, UT Austin, University of Florida, UNIST
- **arXiv**: 2510.03195 (Oct 2025)
- **Dataset**: S&P 100 earnings call transcripts, Jan 2010–Dec 2024, 64 quarters, 5,615 firm-quarter observations

### Core Insight: "Moving Targets"

The paper measures how much companies change the specific metrics they discuss between consecutive earnings calls. Management has discretion over what they emphasize; a company shifting heavily away from previously-highlighted metrics is likely doing so because those metrics are now unflattering.

**Framework**: "LLM as extractor, embedding as ruler"
1. **LLM extracts metric-focused spans**: Gemini-2.5-Pro reads the full earnings call transcript and outputs a structured list of specific performance metrics mentioned (e.g., "North America cloud revenue", "APAC headcount", "gross margin excluding FX")
2. **Embedding similarity measures shift**: text-embedding-3-large computes semantic similarity between the current quarter's metric set and the prior quarter's metric set
3. **Moving targets score**: Higher score = more departure from previously-emphasized metrics

**Key result**: Q5 (highest moving targets) minus Q1 (lowest):
- 5-factor alpha: **-0.52%/month** (t = -2.55, significant at 5%)
- 3-factor alpha: **-0.48%/month** (t = -2.40, significant at 5%)
- Excess return: **-0.41%/month** (t = -2.08)

**Interpretation**: Q5 firms (high metric shifting = more evasive) UNDERPERFORM Q1 firms (consistent metric emphasis = management confident in their business drivers). The alpha is earned by shorting Q5 and being long Q1.

### Why This Is Better Than Keyword/NER Approaches

Traditional NLP uses Named Entity Recognition to count mentions of financial terms. This approach fails because:
- NER misses contextual qualifiers ("North America cloud revenue" vs. "revenue")
- NER can't distinguish "revenue grew 15%" from "we saw some revenue growth"
- NER doesn't capture what management STOPPED discussing

The LLM extraction preserves full context, enabling meaningful comparison.

### Implementation for R28 EarningsQualityAgent

**Required data**: Current quarter transcript + prior quarter transcript (both from EDGAR EFTS API, free)

**Prompt for metric extraction** (use on each transcript separately):
```
Read this earnings call transcript. Extract a list of specific business metrics and KPIs that management discusses, using their exact phrasing. Include quantitative mentions (e.g., 'North America cloud revenue grew 18%') and specific segment/product/geography references. Output a JSON array of metric names. Exclude general terms like 'revenue' or 'earnings' — include only specific, context-rich metrics.
```

**Computing metric consistency score**:
```python
from sklearn.metrics.pairwise import cosine_similarity
import numpy as np

# Embed prior quarter metrics (list of strings) and current quarter metrics
prior_embs = embed(prior_metrics)   # shape: (N, 1536) using text-embedding-3-large
curr_embs = embed(curr_metrics)     # shape: (M, 1536)

# For each prior metric, find best match in current quarter
best_matches = cosine_similarity(prior_embs, curr_embs).max(axis=1)
# Average similarity across all prior metrics
metric_consistency_score = best_matches.mean() * 100  # 0-100
```

**Decision rule**:
- consistency_score < 35: **VETO** — skip PEAD trade
- consistency_score 35–60: **REDUCE** position by 30%
- consistency_score > 60: **No adjustment** — proceed with composite quality score

**Cost**: ~2 API calls per earnings event (one transcript summary per call). At Claude Haiku rates, ~$0.02–0.05 per event. For 30 PEAD events/quarter = ~$1.50/quarter additional cost.

## Focus 2: Strategy-Decay Risk and MRP (arXiv:2604.08356)

### Paper Details
- **Title**: Measuring Strategy-Decay Risk: Minimum Regime Performance and the Durability of Systematic Investing
- **Authors**: Nolan Alexander, Frank Fabozzi
- **Published**: arXiv April 9, 2026 (same day as tonight's dream cycle). Also Journal of Portfolio Management (DOI:10.3905/jpm.2025.1.807)
- **Code**: GitHub (available from authors)

### The MRP Framework

**Minimum Regime Performance (MRP)** = the lowest risk-adjusted return (Sharpe or Calmar ratio) a strategy achieves in any distinct historical market regime.

The framework:
1. Segment historical data into distinct market regimes (bull, bear, high-volatility, low-volatility, etc.)
2. Compute strategy performance within each regime
3. MRP = the minimum across all regime-specific Sharpe ratios

**Key finding**: Strategies with higher long-term Sharpe ratios do NOT necessarily have higher MRP. This reveals an efficiency-resilience trade-off: optimizing for long-run Sharpe can make a strategy brittle in the worst regimes.

### Application to George's Backtest Library

Our top strategies:
- PEAD optimal: Sharpe 4.46 (tested 2018-2024, mostly bull market)
- Div Raise ≥10%, 40d: Sharpe 4.403 (same period)
- Covered calls (pre-ex-div): Sharpe 2.643

All were backtested predominantly in a secular bull market. The MRP question is: how did these strategies perform in:
- **2022 bear market** (S&P down 19.4%, rising rates environment)
- **Q4 2018 selloff** (S&P down 14% in ~3 months)
- **March 2020 crash** (S&P down 34% in ~5 weeks)

For PEAD specifically: in a crash/bear regime, earnings surprises may still create drift (the information mechanism still exists) but position sizing risk is elevated. The RegimeGuard (VIX > 30 skip) approximately handles this by halting new entries during crashes.

For Div Raise strategy: dividend announcements continue in bear markets. The question is whether the 40-day hold period suffers during bear-market drawdowns.

## Focus 3: LLM Extrapolation Bias (arXiv:2604.02921)

### Paper Details
- **Title**: Debiasing LLMs by Fine-tuning
- **Authors**: Zhenyu Gao, Wenxi Jiang, Yutong Yan
- **arXiv**: 2604.02921 (April 2026)
- **Category**: Trading and Market Microstructure (q-fin.TR)

### The Bias

LLMs exhibit systematic extrapolation bias when processing financial data:
- They over-weight recent trends when forming predictions
- In cross-sectional stock return prediction, they load too heavily on recent momentum
- A stock that has been rising is rated higher; a stock that has been falling is rated lower — beyond what fundamentals justify

This extrapolation is the opposite of what event-driven strategies need: PEAD specifically trades stocks AFTER a large move (the earnings gap), which an extrapolating LLM would be inclined to FADE.

### Fix: Anti-Extrapolation Prompt Instruction

Rather than LoRA fine-tuning (which requires labeled data and compute), the zero-cost fix for R28 is an explicit instruction:

```
IMPORTANT INSTRUCTION: You are evaluating the QUALITY of an earnings announcement — whether the beat is genuine, specific, and consistent with prior management guidance. You must NOT consider or infer from the company's recent stock price performance, momentum, or valuation levels. Your evaluation is based solely on the textual content of the filings and transcript excerpts provided. Ignore any financial metrics that reflect market price (P/E, market cap changes, stock return). Focus exclusively on operational and fundamental quality signals.
```

This instruction does three things:
1. Explicitly excludes price/momentum data from the quality assessment
2. Narrows the scope to "operational and fundamental" signals only
3. Anticipates the LLM's tendency to anchor on recent stock performance

### Connection to Existing Heuristics

This builds directly on:
- **"LLM filtering needs RAG context — bare prompts hallucinate judgment"** (heuristics.md): RAG fixes the CONTEXT problem; anti-extrapolation instruction fixes the BIAS problem. Both are needed.
- **"LLM regime blindness (FINSABER validation)"** (heuristics.md): The FINSABER finding was about market TIMING; this is about quality SCORING. Different manifestation of the same underlying problem: LLMs absorb market state into their judgments inappropriately.
