## Key Gaps Identified in Tonight's Scan

### Gap 1: R29 Uses Fixed z-Score Entry/Exit — OU Parameters Not Calibrated Per Pair
Current R29 design uses fixed ±2σ z-score entry / 0 exit for all pairs. But arXiv:2604.02035 (RL for Speculative Trading, April 2026) shows that optimal entry/exit thresholds for OU-spread pairs trading are NOT fixed — they depend on mean reversion speed θ and spread volatility σ.

Key insight from RL closed-form solution:
- High θ (fast mean reversion) → you can enter at TIGHTER spread (±1.5σ) because reversion happens quickly; entering at ±2σ means fewer trades and missed alpha
- Low θ (slow mean reversion) → WIDER threshold needed (±2.5σ) to avoid false signals and drawdown risk
- High σ (noisy spread) → wider threshold needed; low σ → can use tighter entry
- Practical ratio: optimal_threshold ≈ f(σ/√(2θ)) — the OU "equilibrium standard deviation"

Implementation: For each pair, fit OU process via MLE on 60-day spread returns to get (θ, σ, μ). Then set entry_threshold = max(1.5, min(2.5, σ_eq × 2)) where σ_eq = σ/√(2θ). This is ~20 lines of Python.

### Gap 2: R31 Implementation Hazard — Transcript Anonymization Destroys Signal
arXiv:2511.15364 (Anonymization and Information Loss, Nov 2025, q-fin) shows: anonymization of earnings call transcripts destroys more signal than look-ahead bias. Company names, numerical data, product/geography mentions are the highest information-density words. Stripping them for privacy causes pervasive, severe signal loss.

EDGAR transcripts are public — there is NO reason to anonymize. This is a critical implementation warning: when building R31, use raw EDGAR transcripts with FinBERT. Do NOT strip company names, product names, or numerical data as preprocessing.

### Gap 3: R35 Gets a Third Regime Detection Option — Markov Clustered Efficient Frontier
arXiv:2604.03946 introduces regime detection via Markov process on clustered efficient frontier coefficients. Different philosophy from Wasserstein HMM (which works on return distributions):
- EF coefficient clustering detects regimes from PORTFOLIO BEHAVIOR (how mean-variance frontier shifts)
- Wasserstein HMM detects regimes from RETURN DISTRIBUTION changes
- Simple VIX/SMA detects from MARKET INDICATORS

All three are complementary. The EF clustering method has reproducible code available at github.com/nolanalexander/efficient-frontier-coefficients. Could serve as a cheap second regime signal alongside skewness dispersion.
