## Focus 1: SAE Company Similarity for Pairs Trading (arXiv:2412.02605)

### Paper Details
- **Title**: Interpretable Company Similarity with Sparse Autoencoders
- **Authors**: Molinari et al.
- **Published**: ACL 2025 Industry Track, Vienna (peer-reviewed)
- **arXiv**: 2412.02605 (Dec 2024, updated May 2025)

### Architecture Deep Dive

The method uses **EleutherAI/sae-llama-3-8b-32x** — a pre-trained Sparse Autoencoder applied to Llama 3.1 8B hidden states at **layer 30** (approximately 90% model depth, capturing high-level semantic representations via skip connections before logit-specific final layers).

SAE configuration:
- Input: 4,096-dimensional residual stream activations at layer 30
- SAE: TopK activation, k=128 (only 128 features active per company)
- Output: 131,072 sparse features (32x expansion factor)

The SAE decomposes "what the LLM is thinking about when it reads a company description" into 131K interpretable dimensions, of which only ~128 are active per company.

### Key Nuance: When SAE Pre-computed Features May Not Cover Current Stocks
- Pre-computed features from 27,888 reports spanning 1996-2020
- For R29 (running on 2014-2024 data), most S&P 500 constituents should have at least one 10-K in the dataset
- For stocks added to S&P 500 after 2020: use sector ETF as fallback (place in sector cluster)

### Comparison to LLM Economic Plausibility Prompting

| Approach | Cost | Reproducibility | Scale | Performance |
|----------|------|----------------|-------|-------------|
| SAE Clustering | Near-zero (pre-computed) | Deterministic | All pairs simultaneously | Sharpe 12.18 |
| LLM Prompt (arXiv:2602.07048) | ~$0.01-0.05 per pair | Non-deterministic | Sequential API calls | +205% PnL vs baseline |
| SIC Codes | Zero | Deterministic | Instantaneous | Sharpe 9.70 |

**Recommendation**: Use SAE as the fast deterministic pre-filter (Stage 1.5), then LLM prompting as a selective validator on the SMALL set of pairs that pass all prior filters. This gets the best of both: SAE eliminates cheap candidates, LLM validates expensive ones.

## Focus 2: Analyst Belief Biases in Earnings Calls (arXiv:2511.15214)

### Paper Details
- **Title**: Corporate Earnings Calls and Analyst Beliefs
- **arXiv**: 2511.15214 (Nov 2025, v2)
- **Method**: Text morphing via LLM-generated counterfactual transcripts — isolates narrative effects while holding quantitative data constant

### Key Asymmetry Discovered

```
Analysts systematically:
  OVER-REACT to: optimistic framing, confident tone, upbeat language
  UNDER-REACT to: risk language, uncertainty hedging, cautionary phrases
```

This asymmetry is a mispricing opportunity:
- When management says "we're very confident about Q4" — market is likely already pricing this in (over-reaction)
- When management says "we're facing some headwinds in..." — market likely hasn't fully priced this in (under-reaction)

### Practical Implications for R28 EarningsQualityAgent

**Current design (single score)**:
```
quality_score = LLM("Rate earnings quality 0-100")
→ Skip if < 50
```

**Improved design (three-part score)**:
```python
# Q&A section of earnings call (section with highest signal per FinNLP 2025 research)
qa_text = extract_qa_section(transcript)

# Score 1: Specificity (most predictive)
specificity_prompt = """
Rate the specificity of the earnings beat evidence (0-100):
- 0: Vague ('strong momentum across all segments')
- 50: Some specifics ('revenue grew 15%')
- 100: Very specific ('Product A revenue +23%, APAC region +31%, 5 new enterprise clients')
"""

# Score 2: Sentiment (lower weight — analysts already over-incorporate)
sentiment_prompt = """
Rate the optimism/confidence level of management tone (0-100):
- 0: Cautious, hedged language
- 100: Highly confident, forward-looking positive language
"""

# Score 3: Risk density (inverted — analysts under-react to this)
risk_prompt = """
Rate the density of risk/uncertainty language (0-100):
- 0: No risk language, all positive framing
- 100: Heavy risk discussion, multiple headwinds cited
"""

quality_score = 0.5 * specificity + 0.3 * sentiment + 0.2 * (100 - risk_density)
# Note: risk_density is INVERTED — high risk density → lower quality score → skip trade
```

**Expected impact**: More granular filtering should improve signal quality over current single-score design. Specificity captures genuine information content; risk density captures what the market systematically under-prices.

- **Title**: Retail Investor Horizon and Earnings Announcements
- **arXiv**: 2512.00280 (Dec 2025, v2)
- **Data**: StockTwits self-reported holding periods, 2010-2021

### Core Finding

Long-horizon retail investors exhibit **underreaction** to earnings → persistent drift (PEAD)
Short-horizon retail investors exhibit **overreaction** → subsequent reversal (anti-PEAD)

The 0.43%/month zero-cost strategy exploits this heterogeneity.
