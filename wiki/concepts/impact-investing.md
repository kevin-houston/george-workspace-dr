# Impact Investing

## Definition

Investments **intended to deliver positive social or environmental impact alongside financial return**. Distinguished from:
- **Traditional investing**: no explicit impact intent
- **Socially responsible investing (SRI)**: primarily uses negative screening (excludes harmful), doesn't actively pursue positive impact
- **Philanthropy/grants**: capital deployed without financial return expectation

The J.P. Morgan Social Finance Group uses "social" to encompass both social and environmental concerns throughout their research.

---

## Key Characteristics

- **Intentionality**: impact must be the stated purpose of the investment, not a byproduct
- **Measurability**: impact must be tracked (though methods are not yet standardized)
- **Financial return**: some return is expected — distinguishes from grants
- **Range**: from below-market ("concessionary") to market-rate returns depending on mandate

---

## Portfolio Dimensions

Impact investments are evaluated across three co-equal dimensions (see [Three-Dimensional Portfolio Framework](three-dimensional-portfolio-framework.md)):

1. **Impact** — depth and breadth of social/environmental effect
2. **Return** — financial return
3. **Risk** — financial and impact risk

The key insight from J.P. Morgan (2012): these three dimensions are **not inherently in trade-off**. Whether a given deal requires sacrificing return for impact is an empirical question answered by deal-by-deal economic analysis, not by asset-class generalization.

## Organizational Structures for Institutional Impact Investors

| Structure | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Separate team | Dedicated impact team; cleaner mandate; isolation from broader portfolio |
| Hub-spoke | Central impact group overlays impact thesis onto separate instrument-specific teams |
| Whole institution | Entire firm is impact-focused; most coherent mission alignment |

Examples: PGGM (hub-spoke, EUR 4.7bn targeted ESG), F.B. Heron Foundation (whole institution, 100% endowment in impact), Bridges Ventures, MicroVest.

---

## Risks Specific to Impact Investing

Beyond standard investment risks (credit, market, liquidity), impact portfolios face:
- **Mission drift**: investee grows away from its social mission
- **Capital blending**: mixing grant and investment capital requires careful structuring to avoid moral hazard
- **Ecosystem immaturity**: infrastructure (deal sourcing, legal templates, measurement standards) less developed than traditional markets
- **Impact failure recognition**: incentive to not acknowledge when impact goals aren't met

---

## Market Context (2012 baseline)

The J.P. Morgan / GIIN 2011 survey covered 2,200+ private impact transactions across:
- Debt and equity instruments
- Developed and emerging markets
- Multiple sectors (microfinance, affordable housing, clean energy, healthcare, education)

Market was characterized as early-stage / field-building in 2012, with collaborative norms and significant patient capital requirements.
