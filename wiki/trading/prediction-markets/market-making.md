# Market Making & Liquidity Provision on Prediction Markets

Genuinely new gap in this section — `algorithmic-strategies.md`, `automated-pipeline.md`, and
`backtesting-frameworks.md` all cover taking liquidity (directional signals, arbitrage, agent
frameworks); none cover **providing** it. Market making is a structurally different game on
Kalshi/Polymarket than on equities: there's no continuous underlying to delta-hedge against, and
the terminal payoff is binary (0 or 1 at resolution) rather than a continuous price. Both CLOBs
(Kalshi native, Polymarket's Polygon-based CLOB) expose full order-book APIs and maker rebates,
so this is a live, executable strategy class — not just theory.

## Why prediction-market MM differs from equities MM

| Dimension | Equities/options MM | Prediction-market MM |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal value | Continuous (stock price) | Binary — settles to $0 or $1 |
| Hedge instrument | Underlying, correlated assets, options | None — no underlying to hedge; only offsetting positions in the same or a correlated market |
| Inventory risk profile | Roughly symmetric, mean-reverting | Explodes near resolution — a mispriced position held into settlement isn't "wrong by a little," it's binary right/wrong |
| Belief dynamics | Price = expectation of a random walk | Price = market's estimate of P(event); can jump discontinuously on news (a single tweet, a poll release) |
| Typical spread capture | bps on liquid names | Often single-digit-cent spreads (2¢ default proxy used in `prediction-market-backtester`, see `backtesting-frameworks.md`) |

The core practical consequence: a naive symmetric-spread MM strategy that works fine on equities
gets run over near event resolution on prediction markets, because "inventory risk" isn't a
diffusion you can average away — it's a step function.

## Theory: adapting Avellaneda-Stoikov and beyond

### Avellaneda-Stoikov (2008) baseline — still the reference model open-source bots actually use

The classic AS market-making model gives a **reservation price** that skews away from mid based
on current inventory, and an **optimal spread** that widens with time-to-resolution and risk
aversion:

```python
import numpy as np

def avellaneda_stoikov_quotes(mid, inventory, sigma, gamma, k, T_minus_t):
    """
    mid: current mid-price estimate (0-1 probability for PM)
    inventory: current signed position (positive = long YES)
    sigma: volatility of the belief process
    gamma: risk aversion
    k: order arrival intensity decay parameter
    T_minus_t: time remaining to resolution (normalized)
    """
    reservation_price = mid - inventory * gamma * sigma**2 * T_minus_t
    optimal_spread = gamma * sigma**2 * T_minus_t + (2 / gamma) * np.log(1 + gamma / k)
    bid = reservation_price - optimal_spread / 2
    ask = reservation_price + optimal_spread / 2
    return np.clip(bid, 0, 1), np.clip(ask, 0, 1)
```

This is exactly the model `rodlaf/KalshiMarketMaker` (★195, Python) implements against live
Kalshi order flow, with a real-time dashboard. It's the closest thing to a runnable, inspectable
reference for "what does AS look like adapted to a 0-1 bounded price space" — note the manual
`np.clip` at the boundary, which has no equities analog (a stock price doesn't hit a hard 0/1
wall).

**Known gap in the naive adaptation**: AS assumes a continuous diffusion price process with no
terminal jump. On a prediction market, near resolution the "volatility" term should really blow
up (or the model should switch regimes entirely) rather than just shrink `T_minus_t` linearly —
none of the surveyed open-source bots appear to handle this rigorously; it's a design opportunity.

### Optimal Market Making in Prediction Markets (arXiv:2607.17991, 2026)

The rigorous fix for the above gap. Models the market price as **a transformed conditional
probability generated by a latent belief diffusion** (not a raw price diffusion), then solves
the resulting Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation for optimal quotes via a change of variables into
belief-space plus a fixed-point argument (proves existence/uniqueness of the classical solution).

- Optimal quoting strategy is an explicit function of **inventory, current belief, time-to-resolution,
  and risk aversion** — directly generalizes the AS reservation-price structure to the bounded,
  belief-driven setting.
- Numerically, the optimal strategy **"substantially improves downside protection while preserving
  most of its expected profit relative to a myopic benchmark"** (i.e., relative to quoting off the
  raw AS formula without belief-diffusion correction). No specific Sharpe/PnL numbers are given in
  the abstract — would need the full paper for exact figures before treating this as backtest-ready.
- Practical takeaway even without the full derivation: **quote width should scale with
  belief-diffusion volatility, not raw price volatility** — near a 50/50 coin-flip market the two
  coincide, but near a 90/10 market they diverge sharply (small belief moves swing implied price a
  lot less at the extremes under a properly transformed diffusion than a naive linear-price model
  would suggest).

### A General Theory of Liquidity Provisioning for Prediction Markets (arXiv:2311.08725)

Older (2023) but foundational — frames liquidity provision as **submitting/updating an arbitrary
cost function over the entire price space**, not just a fixed bid/ask pair. Generalizes the LMSR
(logarithmic market scoring rule) framework Polymarket/Kalshi's CLOBs implicitly compete against.
Relevant if ever building a market-making bot that posts a full depth ladder rather than a single
best-bid/best-ask pair.

### Predictable Loss (Cartea, Drissi & Monga, SSRN:4273989)

Defines a **comprehensive "predictable loss" metric** for constant-function AMM liquidity
providers (the crypto-AMM analog of impermanent loss, generalized) and derives an optimal LP
strategy from it. **Full formulas are paywalled on SSRN** — the Man Group summary page only gives
author names and the paper's stated goal, not the metric itself. Flagged here as a citation to
chase if we ever build a Polymarket-CLOB-style LP strategy (Polymarket is CLOB not CFMM/AMM, so
direct applicability is limited — more relevant if a future venue uses a constant-function AMM
design).

## Market context (why this might be worth pursuing)

Kalshi processed ~$5.8B in volume in November 2025 alone; Polymarket ~$3.74B — combined monthly
activity approaching $10B. Both platforms pay **maker rebates** for resting limit orders, which
is the direct revenue mechanism MM strategies target (spread capture + rebate, not directional
edge). Professional MM at this scale needs millisecond quote-cancel latency to avoid adverse
selection on stale quotes around news — a meaningfully different infra requirement than this
project's existing daily/monthly-rebalance equity strategies or even the PEAD intraday scanner's
30-minute cadence.

## Relevance to this project

- No current hypothesis targets market-making — H309 (SPX dispersion) and the PM-side hypotheses
  (H185 nowcasting, H316/H319 LLM pairs) are all directional/relative-value, not liquidity
  provision. This is a genuinely open strategy class.
- If pursued, `rodlaf/KalshiMarketMaker`'s AS implementation is the fastest path to a working
  paper-trading prototype; the arXiv:2607.17991 belief-diffusion correction would be the natural
  "why is our naive AS bot getting picked off near resolution" upgrade once a baseline is running.
- Settlement/resolution risk (the binary blow-up near T→0) is the single biggest design
  difference from every other strategy in this wiki — any backtest harness needs an explicit
  "stop quoting inside window W of resolution, or apply the belief-diffusion spread correction"
  rule, not just the standard transaction-cost/slippage models already used elsewhere.
