### New content added

**New page**: `ai-industry/quantsightbench-interval-calibration-2026.md` — covers
QuantSightBench (arXiv:2604.15859, Qin & Andriushchenko, ELLIS/Max Planck, Apr 2026), which
tests whether LLMs produce well-calibrated **prediction intervals for continuous numeric
quantities** (as opposed to FinBench's categorical/classification confidence, already in the
wiki). 11 frontier/open-weight models evaluated; best model (Gemini 3.1 Pro) hit only 79.1%
coverage against a 90% target; coverage degrades sharply at scale (<65% at 100K+ magnitude —
exactly the regime a trading position-sizing decision would care about). No George hypothesis
has yet asked an LLM to output a numeric estimate with an uncertainty band rather than a
bounded classification score, so this is logged as a calibration prior for any future
H279/H280/H281-family proposal that goes that direction, with a note on conformal-prediction
wrapping as the standard (untested here) fix for interval undercoverage.

## Session 2 (later same night) — Wiki Expansion

**Target:** `wiki/dr/` (Disaster Recovery) — re-evaluated the thinnest-section pick from
Session 1 above. DR remains the thinnest section by raw count (5 pages, 624 lines, last
touched 2026-07-27) and, on this pass, a genuinely on-topic research paper surfaced: arXiv
2606.30306 ("Always-On Agents: A Survey of Persistent Memory, State, and Governance in LLM
Agents") is a direct academic treatment of George's own DR problem — persistent agent state,
recoverability, governance — reviewing 435 works and proposing a six-axis framework (authority,
scope, mutability, provenance, recoverability, actionability). Session 1's judgment that DR is
"operational/runbook content, not a research topic" holds for its existing pages, but this
paper is squarely applicable as *theoretical grounding* for why the DR architecture (git +
wiki-as-semantic-layer + diary-as-narrative-layer) is reasonable, and surfaces a concrete gap:
no DR page currently addresses **relinquishing** state (pruning stale wiki/hypothesis content),
which the paper's literature-gap finding calls out as systematically under-addressed across the
field.
