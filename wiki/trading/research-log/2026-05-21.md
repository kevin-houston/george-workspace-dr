## H202-XL — XGBoost 142-Stock Universe (NOT CONFIRMED)

Results: OOS Sharpe 1.050 (6-1m rank), 1.106 (XGBoost). Threshold: 1.5. Reference: H198 (30-stock, 1.174), H202-C (30-stock XGB, 1.278).

**The scaling paradox**: Adding 112 more large-cap stocks *hurts* the signal. The 30-stock universe is dominated by mega-caps (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, META, AMZN, GOOGL) that have strong momentum properties. Adding utilities, consumer staples, materials, and smaller large-caps dilutes the signal pool.

**XGBoost marginal gain persists** (1.106 vs 1.050 rank), consistent with H202-C. The model learns to partially filter out the weaker-signal names, but can't fully compensate for universe dilution.

**Diversification benefit is real**: MaxDD drops from -22.7% (H198) to -14.5% (142-stock rank) — a genuine reduction in idiosyncratic drawdown from more holdings.

**Diagnostic**: The cross-sectional momentum IC (information coefficient) varies strongly by sector. IT/CS stocks have IC ~0.07–0.10; utilities/energy IC is near zero or negative at monthly frequency. A quality filter on universe construction could unlock better results than scaling naively.

High-IVOL stocks in our 30-stock universe are TSLA, AMD, NVDA, META — structural tech winners. The Ang et al. lottery-demand mechanism applies to small-cap speculation, not mega-cap tech. In this universe IVOL is a growth proxy, not a speculative premium.
