### H206 — Halloween Effect Candidate

**Key find**: Schroeder 2025 (IJFS, Nov 2025, doi:10.3390/ijfs13040208) identifies SEC regulatory disclosure seasonality as a structural mechanism for the Halloween effect. From 2004–2023: SEC filings 17% higher in winter (Nov–Apr) vs summer (May–Oct). February = peak month, September = trough. Winter also sees 22% more insider trading, 473% more annual reports, 96% more shareholder meetings. This is a durable, structural driver tied to fiscal-year and regulatory calendars — unlikely to be easily arbitraged.

Confirms H206 is a well-grounded candidate. Updated `calendar-anomalies.md` with this finding and set success gates (H206-A standalone: Sharpe > 0.6; H206-B with TOM: Sharpe > 0.8).

### H202-XL — XGBoost on Large Universe

## H205 Backtest Results — TOM Calendar Overlay on H192-D BAB

**Verdict: NOT CONFIRMED**

Script: `backtesting/daily/run_h205.py` | Results: `backtesting/results/h205_results.json`

### Design

Hold H192-D sector-neutral BAB positions only during TOM windows (last 2 + first 2 trading days per month, ~19% of all trading days). On non-TOM days, return = 0.0 (BIL). The BAB stock selection uses identical monthly beta ranking to H192-D (rolling 252-day OLS vs SPY, rank within GICS sector, long bottom-6).

### OOS Results (2021–2026)

| Strategy | OOS Sharpe | OOS CAGR | MaxDD | NegYrs | Cumul |
|----------|-----------|----------|-------|--------|-------|
| H205 TOM-BAB | 1.177 | 7.8% | −5.4% | 1 | 1.454× |
| H192-D pure BAB | 1.367 | 18.9% | −17.1% | 1 | 2.518× |
| SPY | 0.954 | 14.3% | −23.9% | — | — |

Corr(H205, H192-D) OOS = 0.431

### Confirm Gate

OOS Sharpe > 1.5 → **NOT MET** (1.177). Also below H192-D baseline (1.367).

### Regime Analysis

| Regime | TOM Days | Ann. Return |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| Bull (SPY > 200MA) | 1,062 | 6.7% |
| Bear (SPY ≤ 200MA) | 274 | **13.8%** |

H205 performs better in bear markets — regime-safe property, and the reverse of SPY.

### Key Findings

**1. Proportional scaling, not calendar selectivity.** The TOM window covers ~19% of trading days. The overlay reduces time-in-market by 81%, which scales both realized returns and volatility by ~√5 inversely. Sharpe is approximately preserved (not improved) because BAB alpha is spread uniformly across the month — it is not concentrated in TOM windows. There is no "TOM premium within BAB" to extract.

**2. MaxDD improvement is real but mechanically explained.** By being invested only 19% of the time, the strategy simply avoids 81% of BAB's drawdown days. This is arithmetic, not alpha concentration. MaxDD −5.4% vs −17.1% is a genuine feature for extreme drawdown-controlled mandates, but at the cost of CAGR 7.8% vs 18.9%.

**3. Contrast with H203 hypothesis.** H203 HRP blend showed TOM timing cannot be blended by weight because HRP over-indexes on TOM's low variance. H205 tests TOM as a timing gate. Result: neither approach works because TOM's calendar window does not correspond to when BAB earns its premium. BAB alpha is structural (low-beta premium), not calendar-timed.

**4. Bear regime outperforms bull.** Bear regime ann_ret 13.8% vs 6.7% bull. Possible mechanism: in bear markets, low-beta stocks outperform more strongly per unit of time invested (flight-to-safety effect is front-loaded in month-end liquidity windows). This is the one genuine signal in H205.

### Implication

H205-B variant: apply TOM gate conditionally — full H192-D in bull markets (SPY > 200MA), TOM-gated H192-D in bear markets only. This would attempt to capture the bear-regime outperformance (13.8% ann. return on only 19% of days = very high efficiency per-invested-day) while preserving the full BAB CAGR in bull markets.
