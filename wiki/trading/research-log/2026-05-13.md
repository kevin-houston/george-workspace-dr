## H193: H192-D + H181 Blend — NOT CONFIRMED

Tested sector-neutral BAB (H192-D, Sharpe 1.367) blended with industry reversal (H181, Sharpe 1.138) at 7 weight ratios on the same 30-stock universe.

**Most interesting finding: avg stock overlap = 0.14.** H192-D and H181 select almost entirely different stocks — one picks structurally low-beta names within sectors, the other picks temporarily beaten-down names. These are orthogonal signal dimensions.

## H196: STORM on 90-Stock Universe — NOT CONFIRMED

Hypothesis: expanding STORM from 30 to 90 stocks would unlock dual VQ-VAE orthogonality advantage (more heterogeneous graph connectivity). 

Reality: OOS Sharpe collapsed from 0.963 (H195, 30 stocks) to 0.528 (H196, 90 stocks), failing to beat SPY (0.573). IS/OOS degradation worsened from 41% to 65%.

| Metric | H195 (30 stocks) | H196 (90 stocks) |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|
| IS Sharpe | 1.645 | 1.504 |
| OOS Sharpe | 0.963 | 0.528 |
| IS/OOS decay | 41% | 65% |
| SPY OOS Sharpe | 0.573 | 0.573 |

Root cause: 90 stocks = 8,100 potential graph edges (vs 900 for 30 stocks). Rolling quarterly correlation estimation is noisy at this scale. 84 IS training months insufficient for 90-stock cross-sectional complexity. VQ-VAE codebook quantization becomes more lossy.

**STORM research line closed.** Both H195 and H196 confirm: deep learning does not outperform simple factor models (H191/H192) on US large-cap monthly data.
