## H198: Cross-Sectional Stock Momentum — CONFIRMED

Tested Jegadeesh-Titman (1993) cross-sectional momentum on the same 30-stock large-cap universe used by H181/H192-D.

**Key result: 6-1m lookback dominates, OOS Sharpe 1.174 vs SPY 0.954.**

| Lookback | OOS Sharpe | OOS Cumul | MaxDD   |
|----------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| 12-1m    | 1.096     | 3.376     | -22.6%  |
| **6-1m** | **1.174** | **3.656** | -22.7%  |
| 3-1m     | 0.872     | 2.359     | -26.9%  |
| SPY BH   | 0.954     | 2.044     | -23.9%  |

Surprising finding: bottom-6 (contrarian) OOS Sharpe 1.052 — nearly as good as top-6 (winners). On large-cap stocks, both extremes outperform SPY, suggesting the 30-stock universe is simply "alpha vs. the broad market" rather than clean momentum signal.

Primary limitation: **Corr-SPY = 0.717.** The momentum signal is mostly capturing sector rotation (tech stocks rank together). H026 ETF rotation already exploits this at lower transaction cost.

## H199: Sector-Neutral Momentum — NOT CONFIRMED

Attempted to reduce SPY correlation by applying sector-neutral adjustment (same technique as H181 reversal success). Result: **worse**, not better.

- OOS Sharpe: 0.966 (vs 1.174 raw)
- MaxDD: -37.9% (vs -22.7% raw)
- Corr-SPY: 0.756 (HIGHER, not lower)

Root cause: for momentum, sector drift IS the signal. H181 benefited from sector-neutralization because idiosyncratic reversal (stock vs. its peers) is the actual phenomenon. For momentum, the sector trend itself is the predictive input.

## Key Insight for Stock Momentum Family

On a 30-stock large-cap universe, cross-sectional stock momentum primarily reflects sector rotation. This means:
1. It's confirmed as a signal but redundant with H026 ETF rotation
2. Sector-neutral adjustments hurt (opposite of what helped reversal)
3. A broader universe (200+ stocks across more sectors) would likely show cleaner stock-level idiosyncratic momentum — but requires universe management (survivorship bias, liquidity filters)
