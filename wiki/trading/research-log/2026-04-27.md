**Key takeaway**: VIX spot is the wrong proxy for short-vol returns. Proper implementation requires SVXY daily data or actual CBOE VIX futures settlement prices. This is not feasible without licensed data.

**H031 alone (Sharpe 1.763 on the post-2008 window) remains the best tested portfolio.**

### Interpretation

1. **Commodity momentum exists but is weak.** The signal can distinguish DBA from USO (occasionally selects agriculture, almost never crude), but not with enough conviction to add alpha to the H020 universe.

2. **The vol filter kills USO.** Crude oil's annualized vol (30-50%) means its inverse-vol rank is always near-bottom. Theoretically "strong momentum" in crude can never overcome the vol penalty — by design. This is correct behavior: USO B&H has -47% MaxDD and near-zero return.

3. **DJP is a value trap.** The Bloomberg Commodity Index ETF has structural roll costs (futures-based, paying contango) that erode returns to negative over any long horizon. The momentum signal avoids the worst periods but can't overcome structural negative carry.

4. **GLD in a commodity-only universe is the one bright spot** — but GLD is already in H020 and selected 24.7% of the time there, contributing its alpha without contamination from the weaker commodity assets.

5. **H035 analog**: Just as adding BTC to H020 made BTC never get selected (vol filter excluded it), adding USO to H039a results in USO at 0.9% selection rate. But unlike BTC (which improved Sharpe via vol dilution), USO/DJP don't provide the same signal-dilution benefit — they just add noise.

**Verdict: Both H039a and H039b REJECTED.** The momentum+carry signal does not extend to broad commodity ETFs in a way that improves H020. GLD already captures the "commodity hedge" that works. Adding more commodity exposure via DJP/USO/DBA dilutes returns without reducing risk.

### Why factor ETFs ≈ sector ETFs for this signal

Factor ETFs select stocks from the full US market based on a single characteristic (momentum, quality, value, etc.). Sector ETFs select all stocks in a given industry. Both produce high cross-correlation because:
1. In risk-on markets, all equity factors/sectors outperform bonds simultaneously
2. In risk-off, all fall together
3. The momentum+carry signal selects the "best equity slice" in either case — but both approaches end up with the same broad equity exposure

The signal works better on heterogeneous universes (H020: SPY/QQQ/TLT/GLD/IEF) where assets have genuinely different risk drivers. Factor ETFs are a homogeneous universe.
