## H152 — GDX/SIL Pairs Trading

**NOT CONFIRMED** — Cointegration breaks in OOS period.

### What was tested

- Pair: GDX (gold miners ETF) / SIL (silver miners ETF)
- Data: 2010-04-20 → 2026-04-29 (4,032 trading days)
- OLS hedge ratio on 252-day rolling window (walk-forward, no look-ahead)
- Z-score of spread (60-day lookback), entry ±2σ, exit ±0.5σ, stop ±4σ
- 0.05% round-trip cost

### Cointegration results

| Period | n | p-value | Verdict |
|--------|---|---------|---------|
| Full (2010–2026) | 4032 | 0.4904 | NOT cointegrated |
| IS (2010–2017) | 1940 | 0.0311 | COINTEGRATED ✓ |
| OOS (2018–2026) | 2092 | 0.4650 | NOT cointegrated |

**Root cause**: Gold/silver ratio drifted significantly after 2018. Gold outperformed silver through the 2019–2020 period (gold rallied to ATH while silver lagged), the COVID crash diverged them further, and the 2022 rate cycle hit silver harder (industrial demand collapsed). The ratio stopped mean-reverting to its 2010–2017 equilibrium.

### Performance

| Period | Cumul | CAGR | Sharpe | MaxDD | NegYrs |
|--------|-------|------|--------|-------|--------|
| IS (2011–2017) | 0.59× | −7.3% | −0.360 | −50.8% | 5 |
| OOS (2018–2026) | 0.50× | −8.0% | −0.613 | −58.9% | 6 |
| AltOOS (2013+) | 0.44× | −6.1% | −0.369 | −72.7% | 9 |

SPY OOS (2018+): 3.03× cumulative, 14.3% CAGR, Sharpe 0.741.

**Half-life**: IS 41.3 trading days, OOS 59.3 trading days. Too slow — a ~50-day half-life means the spread takes ~2 months to mean-revert half-way, and full reversion takes even longer. Z-score entries fire infrequently (47% of OOS days in position) but the regime changes between entries and exits.

**Correlation with H026**: OOS −0.102, AltOOS −0.011. Near-zero as expected for a market-neutral strategy — this part works perfectly.

### Key takeaway

The cointegration breakdown (not the strategy logic) is the failure. The rolling OLS hedge ratio is adjusting to the new regime but the fundamental relationship between GDX and SIL changed structurally. This is the **XLE/OIH 2020 breakdown** analog described in pairs-trading.md — sector composition drift changes the fundamental link.

**NOT CONFIRMED.** Not cointegrated in any window (full p=0.99, IS p=0.77, OOS p=0.96).

Root cause: post-2014 shale revolution and post-2020 energy transition permanently decoupled oil services from E&P capex cycles. XLE (majors: XOM, CVX, EOG) outperformed OIH (services: SLB, HAL, BKR) by 3-4× over 2012-2026.

Do not retry this pair. Economic link is broken.

## H154 — TLT/IEF Treasury Yield Curve Pair

**NOT CONFIRMED** — but most promising pairs result yet.

Three z-score variants tested (30d, 60d, 120d lookback):

| Variant | OOS Sharpe | OOS Cumul | OOS MaxDD | OOS NegYrs |
|---------|-----------|----------|----------|-----------|
| A (z_lb=30d) | 0.514 | 1.226× | −7.18% | 3 |
| B (z_lb=60d) | 0.252 | 1.104× | −10.89% | 2 |
| C (z_lb=120d) | 0.334 | 1.138× | −7.12% | 4 |

SPY OOS: 3.03× cumulative, Sharpe 0.741.

**Not formally cointegrated** (full p=0.967, IS p=0.336, OOS p=0.946). But spread has genuine short-term mean reversion: half-life ~33d (full), ~30d (OOS). The yield curve spread fluctuates but doesn't hold a fixed long-run equilibrium — it drifts as monetary cycles shift.

**Why cointegration fails**: The formal Engle-Granger test fails because both TLT and IEF prices have been trending (yields trended down 2007-2021, then sharply up 2022). Their price ratio isn't mean-reverting over the full period. But at 30-60 day horizons, yield curve slope fluctuations do mean-revert.

**Key insight**: Variant A works but doesn't beat SPY. A Kalman filter (adaptive β) could help by tracking the changing duration ratio as rates move — the static OLS β of 1.51 may be too rigid.
