## H202 — ML-Enhanced Cross-Sectional Momentum

**Result: NOT CONFIRMED** (threshold OOS Sharpe > 1.474 not met)

Three variants on the 30-stock S&P 500 universe, IS 2013–2020, OOS 2021–2026:

| Variant | OOS Sharpe | OOS Cumul | MaxDD | Note |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-------|------|
| A: Simple 6-1m rank (H198 baseline) | 1.174 | 3.66× | -22.7% | Reproduces H198 exactly |
| B: Bias-masked rank | 1.177 | 3.68× | -21.4% | Trivial lift; bias mask rarely fires |
| C: XGBoost multi-factor | **1.278** | **4.94×** | **-20.0%** | Genuine +0.104 Sharpe lift |

SPY OOS: 0.954, 2.04×

**Key findings:**
- The bias mask concept from arXiv:2507.07107 barely applies to 30 large-cap S&P 500 stocks — these never show >25% monthly moves (the exclusion threshold), so the mask fires on only 1.6 stocks per month on average.
- XGBoost with 8 features (mom_6_1, mom_12_1, mom_3_1, rev_1m, vol_12m, vol_1m, vol_change, mom_risk_adj) genuinely outperforms the simple rank: +0.104 Sharpe OOS, +35% cumulative wealth.
- Corr(XGBoost, simple rank) OOS = 0.783 — XGBoost selects materially different stocks, not just re-ranking the same candidates.
- Root cause for not confirming: 30 stocks is too small a universe for ML cross-sectional alpha. The +0.44 Sharpe gain in the paper requires 500+ stocks where the signal-to-noise is better.

**H202-XL queued:** test XGBoost on a 200-stock universe (Russell 200 equivalent) where bias mask and ML differentiation can have more impact.

## H203 — HRP Multi-Strategy Blend

**Result: NOT CONFIRMED** (OOS Sharpe 1.066 < threshold 1.2)

Blended S1=6-1m momentum, S2=hybrid low-vol, S3=TOM timing via HRP with 24-month Ledoit-Wolf covariance. IS: 2016–2021, OOS: 2022–2026.

| Strategy | OOS Sharpe | OOS Cumul | MaxDD |
|----------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| S1: 6-1m Momentum | 1.080 | 2.72× | -15.5% |
| S2: Hybrid Low-Vol | 0.570 | 1.39× | -13.9% |
| S3: TOM (SPY timing) | 0.733 | 1.28× | -11.0% |
| HRP Blend | 1.066 | 1.46× | **-7.1%** |
| Equal-weight 1/3 | 1.051 | 1.74× | -9.5% |
| SPY | 0.743 | 1.59× | -20.2% |

OOS correlations: Corr(S1,S2)=0.635, Corr(S1,S3)=0.190, Corr(S2,S3)=0.312

**Key finding — HRP overweights TOM (74% avg OOS weight):** TOM has the lowest variance (only invested ~19% of trading days) and lowest correlation with S1 and S2. HRP is a variance-minimizing algorithm — it rationally allocates most capital to the lowest-variance asset regardless of CAGR. This suppresses portfolio returns drastically.

**The MaxDD improvement is striking:** -7.1% for HRP blend vs -15.5% for pure momentum. If Kevin's goal were maximizing Sharpe-per-unit-drawdown rather than absolute returns, the blend would be attractive.

**Root cause:** HRP is well-suited for blending strategies with similar return profiles. When one strategy (TOM, CAGR ~5%) has much lower variance than others (momentum, CAGR ~18%), HRP excessively concentrates in the low-variance/low-return strategy.

**Implication — H205 queued:** Rather than blending strategies by portfolio weight, test a TOM calendar OVERLAY: hold H192-D BAB positions only during TOM windows (last 2 + first 2 trading days), hold BIL otherwise. This preserves the BAB alpha while adding TOM's calendar timing, without letting HRP over-index on variance.
