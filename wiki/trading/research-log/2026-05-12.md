# Research Log — May 12, 2026

## H192: Betting Against Beta (BAB) — CONFIRMED

Ran 4-variant BAB backtest on the same 30-stock large-cap universe used for H191 (low-volatility).

| Variant | Sharpe | CAGR | MaxDD |
|---------|--------|------|-------|
| A: Raw OLS beta | 0.943 | 12.1% | -19.8% |
| B: Vasicek-shrunk (0.6/0.4) | 0.943 | 12.1% | -19.8% |
| C: Hybrid 50% beta + 50% vol rank | 1.089 | 13.7% | -18.2% |
| D: Sector-neutral beta | **1.367** | 16.4% | -17.1% |

**Key finding — BAB ≈ Low-Vol on this universe**: Corr(H192-A, H191-A) = 0.799. Both strategies select the same defensive stocks (JNJ, WMT, COST, IBM). Deploying H192 alongside H191 provides negligible diversification.

**Why sector-neutral wins**: H192-D ranks beta within each GICS sector, selecting the calmest stock per sector rather than always picking defensive sectors. This breaks the BAB/Low-Vol equivalence (corr drops to 0.723) and adds genuine cross-sector coverage.

**Vasicek shrinkage null result**: On 30 stocks, 0.6×beta + 0.4×1.0 doesn't change relative rankings — A ≡ B. Expected on small universe.

**Portfolio decision**: Don't deploy H192 alongside H191. The two signal redundantly. H192-D is the superior strategy on its own merits (Sharpe 1.367 vs H191-C Sharpe 1.110).
